## Key findings
- Average meat supply per person rose **77%** between 1961 and 2017 (27.6 kg to 48.7 kg).
- **Poultry** grew from 9.8% to 41.3% of the meat supply, overtaking beef.
- Markets are very unequal: Hong Kong has about **36 times** India's meat supply per person (137.1 kg against 3.8 kg).
- The share of countries in severe food deficit fell from **45.2% to 4.7%** (1961 to 2017), but 8 countries are still flagged and 33 more are on a watch list.
- Algeria's milk supply rose about **3 times**, and Brazil's poultry supply about **28 times**.

## Strategic recommendations (Week 5)
1. Prioritise poultry processing capacity.
2. Build a shelf-stable dairy offer for markets leaving food scarcity.
3. Sort markets into three tiers and set targets by market, not by the global average.
4. Run the annual early-warning list and plan affordable, fortified products.
5. Make data-quality checks and automation standard practice.
6. Collect plant-level data to measure real efficiency.

## Data source
FAO Food Balance Sheets (FAOSTAT), obtained through the Our World in Data repository on GitHub ("Food supply (FAO, 2020)"). Please credit FAO and Our World in Data if you reuse the data.

## What is real and what is illustrative
The analysis of the FAO data is real. The Production Efficiency Index, hypothesis tests H1 to H3 and the dollar figures in the Week 1 plan are **illustrative examples** that show how the method works, and the reports label them as such. The data measures food **supply** per person, not food eaten or factory output.
